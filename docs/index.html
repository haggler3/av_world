What this does that common tools don't

Driving simulation has split into log replay (realistic but bounded by its logs) and self-play (scales, but every agent is the policy). av_world sits in between: a synthetic, open-ended city whose human drivers make the mistakes real humans make, at the rates they make them.

01 · Calibrated risk

Crashes by type, matched to real data

Red-light runners, drivers who don't look, distraction and breakdowns are tuned so rear-end, angle, left-turn and sideswipe rates match published intersection statistics. Most simulators are collision-free by default or inherit their logs.

02 · The simulator as the variable

Experiments on the teacher

The dynamics ladder turns on one source of dynamics at a time and measures what it buys the learner. Active scenario selection asks which simulated data teaches fastest.

03 · One stack

Sim → RL → world model → scaling laws

The same simulator logs train multi-agent PPO and diffusion / flow-matching world models, so their scaling laws can be compared directly, in one repository.

04 · Any city

From open map data to a live neighbourhood

Lanes, junction boxes, conflict points, signals, stop signs, crosswalks and demand built automatically from Overture Maps / OpenStreetMap. Oakland, Squirrel Hill and Downtown ship ready.

05 · Long horizons

Minutes to hours, not 9-second snippets

Traffic forms, queues spill back, buses bunch, vans double-park and drivers reroute around congestion. The AV meets the consequences of its own behaviour.

06 · Inspectable

Every key moment, one click away

The browser viewer indexes each crash, red run and breakdown, so you can jump straight to it, follow any AV and watch the area from three camera views.

Results

Every experiment is a set of jobs of five minutes or less on four CPU cores. Every policy is scored in the same held-out world with all dynamics on and humans 5× riskier than calibrated.

Does a more dynamic world train a better driver?

Five training worlds on one map, each adding a source of dynamics: AVs alone → human traffic → pedestrians → buses, vans and breakdowns → risky humans.

  • +12 % score for the full world after 7,800 steps, starting from imitation.
  • +20 % from scratch: with no imitation start, the world is the only teacher.
  • Every world converges by ~60 k steps. Dynamics buy sample efficiency; steps set the ceiling.

Scaling laws across architectures

World models trained on 2.8 M simulated AV decisions; error = a·x−b fitted in log-log space. The driving policy's score follows a power law in simulator steps too.

  • Compute exponents 0.11–0.17 for MLPs and flow matching (R² > 0.9).
  • A 1 M-parameter MLP gains 2× more per unit of simulated data than a 0.2 M one: capacity decides how much simulation helps.
  • RL score vs steps: b ≈ 0.77, the same law in the simplest and the full world.

Active learning: which simulated data teaches fastest?

54 scenarios (demand × human risk × pedestrians × AV share). Uniform sampling vs prioritised level replay, failure-focused replay, and a critic warm-up, all at an equal budget of simulator steps.

  • Failure-focused replay leads at mid-budget (+2.6 % at 31 k steps), and all three selection rules finish within 2 %.
  • The least informative scenario still carries 73 % of the learning signal of the most informative one, so every corner of the city still teaches.
  • At this stage the lever is the volume of dynamic simulation; selection pays once the common cases are mastered.

AVBench: the bar the learned driver has to clear

Nine scenarios (rush hour, red runners, inattentive humans, jaywalkers, blockages, an 80 % AV world and two unseen neighbourhoods) × eight checks, each against the rule-based AV driving the same traffic. Every policy is scored as deployed, with the safety envelope on, and as raw RL output, with it off.

  • As deployed, the best policy has 3.7× fewer crashes and 5× fewer hard brakes than the rule-based AV, at the same speed.
  • Unaided, no policy overcomes a scenario yet. The 62k-step policies crash 45–57 times per AV-hour: they accelerate and let the envelope brake, which the training score cannot see.
  • A 10× envelope penalty removes raw crashes but halves the speed. The bar has two sides, and 72 checks to clear.

Where this sits in current research

The field's 2023–2025 trends, and the question this project asks of each.

Scale beats hand-tuning

Gigaflow (ICML 2025) trained one self-play policy on 1.6 billion km and set the state of the art on CARLA, nuPlan and Waymax without human data. Here: the same bet at laptop scale, with calibrated humans as the hard part of the world.

Throughput is the bottleneck

GPUDrive (ICLR 2025) runs over a million steps per second on a GPU. Here: jitted end to end with fixed shapes, already 380× real time on CPU and ready to move to a GPU.

Scaling laws for driving

Waymo (2025) found power laws in compute for motion forecasting and planning. Here: the same laws for policy score vs simulator steps and for world-model error vs compute and data, across four architectures.

Generative world models

GAIA-2 (Wayve, 2025) generates multi-camera driving video with latent diffusion. Here: diffusion and flow matching on the traffic state itself, sampling 3 s futures in 0.06 ms.

Data-driven simulation in JAX

Waymax (NeurIPS 2023) made log-replay simulation batched and accelerator-native. Here: the same pure-step, vmapped-worlds style, but the world generates its own traffic.

Adaptive curricula

Prioritised level replay picks training environments by learning potential. Here: tested head-to-head against uniform sampling on a 54-scenario city grid.

Rust · city scale

small_av_world

A deterministic city traffic simulator in Rust: 1,500 road segments, 400 signalised intersections, ~70,000 trips a day following a real daily rhythm, congestion-aware routing and incidents. A full day in ~14 s on one core (~6,000× real time), with a browser viewer.

small_av

A complete classical AV stack on CPU, from raw LiDAR to steering and throttle: perception → tracking → prediction → behaviour → planning → control. Includes a forecasting model trained on Argoverse 2 and an ablation of which parts of the stack matter.

What's next

  1. Overcome AVBench unaidedAnneal the envelope penalty, or train with the envelope off and a large crash penalty, from the 234k-step policy. Target: all 72 checks.
  2. Scale the simulation on a GPUTest the fitted power laws 4–16× further out (62 k → 1 M simulator steps); the jitted step moves unchanged.
  3. Train the driver inside the world modelThe diffusion model samples a 3 s future in 0.06 ms. Use imagined rollouts (Dreamer-style) and compare steps-to-score with the dynamics ladder.
  4. Failure-focused replay at scaleThe failure score separates scenarios far more than the learning signal. Oversample where AVs still brake hard or nearly collide, and mine new scenarios from the viewer's key moments.
  5. One policy, many neighbourhoodsTrain across Oakland, Squirrel Hill and Downtown and measure transfer to an unseen part of the city.
  6. Close the loop with RustPort the 28-feature observation to small_av_world so the city-area policy runs in the Rust AV stack, as the corridor driver already does.
