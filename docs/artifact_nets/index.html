Only-in-Simulation Nets

PPO and next-step world models can be trained in any simulator. These six networks need three things this one has: every driver's hidden traits in the state, forks of the whole city that differ in one chosen thing with every other die held fixed, and thousands of worlds with known settings next to 400 real Argoverse 2 scenes.

N2

An interventional risk critic

works

Fork the city at a moment, give the AVs five different actions for 2 s, replay 4 s with the same dice. A critic trained on those forks chooses well. The same network trained on the log is confounded, and does worse than not intervening at all.

Q(o, a) = P( crash, near miss or more than 1 m inside the safety barrier, in 4 s | o, do(a) )

interventional   every state × all 5 actions, from the forks         (560,124 AV-moments × 6 branches, 238 s to simulate)
observational    every state × the one action the policy took (the log)
Latin square     AV i in branch b takes action (b + hᵢ) mod 5, so neighbours do not all act alike

Confounding, measured. In the log the policy accelerates only where it is safe to, so the observational critic thinks accelerating is harmless: its predicted effect is −0.02 points, the true effect +3.5. The interventional critic’s predicted risk changes correlate 0.74 with the true paired changes; the observational critic’s correlate −0.01.

N7

How the human behind answers

works

With shared dice, the follower’s acceleration in a branch minus its acceleration in the unperturbed branch is the causal effect of the AV’s action. A network trained on those differences predicts it. A network trained on the log does worse than assuming the follower ignores us.

δₖ(t) = a_follower(t | do(aₖ)) − a_follower(t | nobody perturbed),     t = 0.2 … 3.8 s        168,342 AV-moments with a human behind
N6

Seeing through the bus

works

Every vehicle’s true position is known, so for every AV the simulator can label exactly who is hidden behind whom. A network that sees only what the AV sees learns where the hidden road users are.

hidden  ⇔  the sight line from the AV to its centre crosses another vehicle’s footprint (slab test on oriented boxes), within 40 m
net     visible road users (position, heading, speed, length, kind) → deep set → 8 × 8 grid of P(hidden here) + P(hidden 25 m ahead)
N1

The mind reader

style yes, intent no

From 10 s of a car’s ordinary driving, a causal convolution reads its driving style: headway, acceleration, desired speed. It cannot tell a red-light runner or an aggressive driver from anyone else.

N5

But-for blame

mixed

Rewind every crash 6–8 s and replay it once per nearby driver, each time with that driver’s mistakes off, then with that driver driving carefully. Crashes almost never hinge on one mistake. Half hinge on someone’s ordinary driving style.

Reading it. For “who could have avoided it”, the faster of the two vehicles is a strong rule: it names one driver better than the network (top-1 0.95 vs 0.89), while the network ranks everyone better (AUROC 0.94 vs 0.91). Knowing who made a mistake is a poor guide to who could have prevented the crash (top-1 0.47). The mistake question has 12 test crashes, too few to conclude.

N4

Which settings explain real traffic?

diagnoses the gap

Simulate 1,024 worlds with random settings, summarise each the way real logs can be summarised, and train a network to map statistics to a posterior over settings. Pointed at real Argoverse 2, the posterior beats a point fit, and shows that the simulator cannot reach real traffic.

q(θ | s) = Σ_{c=1..4} π_c(s) · N( logit u ; μ_c(s), diag σ_c²(s) )        u = (θ − θ_min) / (θ_max − θ_min)
θ (9)   p_red, distraction, p_aggressive, T_med, T_sigma, acceleration noise, brake pulses, pedestrian volume, p_ped_violate
s (6)   hard brakes /h, headway < 0.8 s, TTC < 2 s, TTC < 1 s, median speed, acceleration SD
Posterior-predictive check, 32 fresh copiesDistance to real statistics (SD)
posterior draws2.24 [2.17, 2.33]
CEM point fit (tuned to four of the six statistics)2.60 [2.51, 2.68]
prior draws3.50 [3.34, 3.66]

Misspecification, found. Tight following, TTC under 1 s and acceleration SD in real traffic lie outside the range of every simulated world. The posterior therefore piles onto the edge of the box: shortest headway, most acceleration noise. Its tight intervals there are extrapolation, not knowledge. Within this prior, the simulator’s drivers cannot follow as closely, or brake as late, as real ones.
